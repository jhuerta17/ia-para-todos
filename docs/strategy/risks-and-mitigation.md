# Risks and Mitigation

> **DRAFT:** Living risk register. Review after each validation activity and pilot workshop; add newly observed risks. All mitigations are proposed, not yet implemented or tested.

## 1. Lack of validated community demand

**Risk:** We build curricula for needs the community doesn't actually prioritize. Enthusiasm from one or two contacts is not evidence of demand.

**Mitigation:**

- Interview ~10 prospective participants before finalizing any curriculum.
- Validate topics through short conversations or surveys first; write materials second.
- Treat the pilot as a demand test with explicit go/no-go criteria, not a launch event.

## 2. Low workshop attendance

**Risk:** Interest from a host organization does not guarantee attendance. Enthusiastic hosts and empty rooms can coexist.

**Mitigation:**

- Promote through hosts' existing channels (bulletins, chats, announcements), not ours.
- Schedule around community availability (evenings, weekends); send reminders.
- Track registration-to-attendance conversion; investigate drop-off causes early.
- Start small (10–20 participants) so a soft turnout is a lesson, not a failure.

## 3. Digital literacy barriers

**Risk:** Some participants may need help with basic device operation, accounts, passwords, or internet navigation — blocking the actual AI curriculum.

**Mitigation:**

- Run a simple readiness check at registration (can you open a browser? do you have an email account?).
- Offer an optional digital-skills orientation before Workshop 1.
- Write smartphone-first, step-by-step instructions; never assume laptops.
- Provide alternatives when account creation isn't practical (facilitator demo device, shared walkthrough, paper handouts).
- Staff assistant facilitators to help strugglers without stopping the group.

## 4. Founder dependency and burnout

**Risk:** The project permanently depends on one person teaching, organizing, and deciding everything — fragile, unscalable, and a path to burnout.

**Mitigation:**

- Build reusable curricula with complete facilitator notes from day one.
- Recruit and train at least one assistant facilitator during the pilot.
- Document repeatable procedures (checklists, outreach steps, budgets) instead of holding them in one head.
- Measure bench strength (trained volunteers), not just workshops delivered.
- Cap pilot commitments explicitly; say no to expansion that requires the founder in the room.

## 5. Limited funding

**Risk:** Free workshops still cost money — materials, transport, supplies, insurance, software, equipment, accessibility support. Unfunded effort degrades quality and burns out volunteers.

**Mitigation:**

- Keep fixed costs near zero until demand is proven; fund the pilot out of pocket at small scale.
- Measure true cost per workshop and per participant during the pilot.
- Explore fiscal sponsorship before independent incorporation (see `../nonprofit/` — planned).
- Pursue only mission-aligned funding; never accept conditions requiring paid tools, vendor favoritism, or exclusionary eligibility.

## 6. Unreliable internet or equipment

**Risk:** Venue Wi-Fi fails, participants' phones can't run a tool, the projector dies — and a hands-on workshop becomes a lecture, or nothing.

**Mitigation:**

- Assess every venue in advance: Wi-Fi reliability, outlet count, seating, lighting.
- Bring a mobile hotspot and offline-capable backup activities (paper exercises, guided discussion).
- Design exercises that tolerate low bandwidth; test them on low-end phones.
- Carry adapters, extension cords, a speaker, and at least one backup demo device.

## 7. Lack of community trust

**Risk:** Participants and partners may distrust AI itself, fear for their privacy, or question outsiders offering "free" technology help.

**Mitigation:**

- Partner only with organizations the community already trusts.
- Use approachable, jargon-free language in the community's language.
- Demonstrate practically; avoid hype; communicate AI limits honestly.
- Keep workshops nonsectarian and non-commercial, and say so explicitly.
- Never collect sensitive personal data; explain plainly what anonymous feedback is and isn't used for.

## 8. AI misinformation and hallucinations

**Risk:** Participants act on confident-sounding but wrong AI output — in health, legal, financial, or school contexts — or spread AI-generated misinformation.

**Mitigation:**

- Teach AI limitations explicitly in every workshop: confident tone ≠ correct answer.
- Practice fact-checking and independent verification as a core skill, not a footnote.
- Use fictional examples in exercises; never real sensitive scenarios.
- Provide a take-home one-page reminder reinforcing verification habits.

## 9. Privacy and security risks

**Risk:** Participants paste personal, family, financial, or immigration-related information into AI tools, or fall for scams and unsafe defaults.

**Mitigation:**

- Teach "what never to share with AI tools" in every session, with concrete examples.
- Never use real sensitive information during exercises.
- Cover account basics: passwords, phishing awareness, app permissions.
- Keep this repository free of any participant data (see [../../CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) privacy rules).

## 10. Inconsistent volunteer instruction

**Risk:** Technically knowledgeable volunteers may not be effective instructors; inconsistent delivery confuses learners and damages trust.

**Mitigation:**

- Require facilitator training with practice sessions before solo delivery.
- Use consistent written lesson plans — no freelancing the core curriculum.
- Define volunteer roles and responsibilities clearly.
- Pair new facilitators with experienced ones; review participant feedback with facilitators after every workshop.

## 11. Expanding too quickly

**Risk:** Attempting churches, schools, libraries, and employers simultaneously before proving the initial program is repeatable.

**Mitigation:**

- Follow phased growth with explicit graduation criteria (see [growth-strategy.md](growth-strategy.md)).
- One new venue type at a time; say no to opportunities that skip validation steps.
- Measure repeatability (can a non-founder deliver it?) before measuring reach.

## 12. Inadequate measurement of learning outcomes

**Risk:** Counting heads instead of learning — declaring success from attendance while participants leave unable to do anything new.

**Mitigation:**

- Define outcomes before the pilot: exercises completed, confidence change, 30-day reuse.
- Collect anonymous before/after confidence ratings and in-session completion checks.
- Treat targets as experimental hypotheses, not marketing claims; publish misses alongside hits.
- Keep measurement privacy-preserving: aggregate, anonymous, never stored in this repo.

## 13. Potential conflicts of interest involving separate commercial businesses

**Risk:** The founder has separate professional and commercial activities in technology. Without safeguards, the initiative could appear — or become — a marketing channel rather than an independent educational program.

**Mitigation:**

- Keep the initiative independent from commercial lead generation: no attendee lists shared, no sales pitches, no product favoritism.
- Any future transactions involving related businesses would require appropriate disclosure, independent evaluation, recusal, and organizational safeguards.
- Adopt a written conflict-of-interest policy before any formalization (see `../nonprofit/` — planned).
- Curricula recommend categories of free tools, never a single vendor, and never a related business.

> No names, financial information, or confidential details about the founder's businesses are included in this repository, by design.
