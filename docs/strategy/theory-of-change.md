# Theory of Change

> **DRAFT:** Initial causal model. Pilot data should confirm, refute, or refine each link. Nothing below is a claim of results — no workshops have been held.

## In one sentence

If underserved Spanish-speaking adults receive free, hands-on, smartphone-first AI workshops at trusted venues — with explicit teaching on accuracy, privacy, and verification — then they could gain usable skills and critical habits that improve daily life, work, and family technology guidance.

## The proposed causal chain

1. **Inputs**
   - Volunteer facilitators trained on consistent lesson plans.
   - Tested Spanish-first curricula requiring only smartphones and free tools.
   - Trusted host venues (churches initially; libraries, centers, schools later).
   - Modest funding for materials, transport, supplies, and accessibility.

2. **Activities**
   - 90–120 minute workshops with live demos and guided practice.
   - Optional digital-skills orientation for participants needing device basics.
   - Anonymous feedback collection after every session.
   - Facilitator debriefs and curriculum revision cycles.

3. **Outputs**
   - Workshops delivered; participants attending; exercises completed in-session.

4. **Short-term outcomes (0–30 days, hypothesized)**
   - Participants complete at least one practical AI task during the workshop.
   - Self-reported confidence in using AI increases.
   - Participants can name at least one AI limitation and one privacy precaution.

5. **Medium-term outcomes (1–12 months, hypothesized)**
   - Participants report using AI again after 30 days for real tasks.
   - Parents report feeling better equipped to guide children's technology use.
   - Host organizations request repeat or follow-up sessions.

6. **Long-term impact (aspired)**
   - Reduced digital divide in served communities: broader, safer, more critical AI use.
   - A volunteer facilitator pipeline that outlasts any single founder.
   - A replicable, documented model other communities could adopt.

## Key assumptions (to test)

- Community members will attend free AI workshops at trusted venues.
- 90–120 minutes suffices for a beginner to complete one practical task.
- Smartphone-only participation is viable for the chosen exercises.
- Volunteers can be recruited and trained to facilitate consistently.
- Anonymous feedback yields honest, usable signal.
- Host organizations will provide space, outreach help, and basic logistics.
