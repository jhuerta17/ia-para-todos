# Governance Considerations

> **DRAFT — INFORMATIONAL, NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Future-state planning only. No board, bylaws, or legal entity exist today. These notes describe what good governance would require if the initiative formalizes — they do not constitute governance.

## Why plan governance now

Governance built after problems arise is damage control. These considerations let the pilot phase operate with lightweight discipline (clear decisions, written records, separated money) so a future entity — if ever formed — inherits good habits, not hidden liabilities.

## Board of directors (future)

- A Texas nonprofit corporation would need a board meeting state minimums — **verify current requirements** with the Texas Secretary of State rather than assuming a number.
- Proposed composition principles: majority independent community members (not family, not business partners of the founder); representation of the communities served (Spanish-speaking DFW residents); staggered terms to avoid total turnover; term limits to prevent entrenchment.
- The founder may serve initially but must not be irremovable or solely empowered: no single-signature financial authority, no unchecked executive role.
- Until a board exists, major decisions should still be written down with date, rationale, and who agreed (see Recordkeeping).

## Bylaws (future)

Future bylaws should address at minimum: purpose consistent with the mission; board size, terms, and removal; officer roles; meeting frequency and quorum; voting and conflict-of-interest procedures; financial controls; amendment process; dissolution clause directing assets to a compatible charitable purpose. Use a Texas-specific nonprofit template reviewed by a qualified attorney — do not copy another state's bylaws.

## Organizational decision-making

- **Now (informal):** founder-led with documented decisions; seek input from volunteers and host partners; disclose the exploratory status in every external commitment.
- **Later (sponsored or independent):** decisions within board-approved policy; spending above a threshold requires dual authorization; program changes follow evidence from [pilot retrospectives](../../templates/pilot-retrospective.md), not personal preference.
- Bright line: no commitment of funds, partnerships, or legal obligations beyond what the current operating model supports.

## Financial oversight

- Separate any project money from personal funds as early as possible (separate account once any donations exist).
- Receipts for every expense; monthly reconciliation; annual summary shared with stakeholders.
- Dual authorization above a defined threshold (amount to be set if funds materialize).
- No cash payments without receipts; no reimbursements without prior approval for non-trivial amounts.
- Independent review (or audit, if scale requires) before any public financial claims. See [budget-template.md](../operations/budget-template.md).

## Transparency

- Publish aggregate, anonymized results: workshops held, attendance, completion and confidence signals, cost per participant.
- Publish the conflict-of-interest policy and confirm annually that it is followed.
- Never publish individual feedback, donor identities (without consent), or anything identifying participants or partners.
- Correct the public record promptly when plans change or targets are missed.

## Succession planning

The initiative must survive its founder. Concretely: everything needed to run a workshop already lives in this repository ([operations](../operations/) + [workshops](../../workshops/)); add a private continuity note (account access, key contacts, pending obligations — stored privately, never here) naming at least one successor volunteer; cross-train facilitators so no role has a single holder.

## Recordkeeping

Keep, privately and durably: decision log; meeting notes (without personal data); financial records and receipts (retain per future accountant/attorney guidance); volunteer agreements; host correspondence and venue agreements; insurance policies; feedback tallies (aggregate only). Retention periods and formats to be confirmed with professional advisors if the project formalizes.

## Conflicts of interest

Full treatment in [conflict-of-interest.md](conflict-of-interest.md). Governance summary: annual disclosure by decision-makers; recusal from related decisions; independent review of any related-party transaction; absolute separation between the initiative and any commercial lead generation.

## Volunteer responsibilities

Volunteers (see [volunteer-onboarding.md](../operations/volunteer-onboarding.md)) are responsible for: delivering the written curriculum; protecting participant privacy; following conduct expectations; reporting safety or ethical concerns. They are not responsible for organizational debts, legal compliance of the entity, or decisions above their role — those sit with the organizers (now) or the board (later).
