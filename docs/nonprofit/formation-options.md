# Formation Options

> **DRAFT — INFORMATIONAL, NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Early-stage exploration only. No legal entity exists, no option has been selected, and no filings have been made. Verify every legal claim against official sources (see [compliance-checklist.md](compliance-checklist.md)) before acting.

## Current status

IA Para Todos is an **informal, early-stage community initiative**. It is not an incorporated nonprofit, not an IRS-recognized 501(c)(3), not a registered charity, and not a tax-exempt organization. No document in this repository claims otherwise.

## Working recommendation

**Validate the educational program through pilot workshops before committing significant resources to creating an independent legal entity.** Legal structure should follow proven demand — not precede it. The likely sequence: operate informally during validation → consider fiscal sponsorship once demand and costs justify it → consider independence only if scale and sustainability warrant it.

## Option 1 — Informal volunteer initiative (current state)

Operate as an unincorporated group of volunteers with no legal entity.

**Advantages:** zero formation cost or paperwork; maximum flexibility; appropriate for small pilots funded out of pocket; easy to pause or reshape based on evidence.

**Limitations:** no legal personality (cannot sign contracts, hold bank accounts, or receive grants as an organization); no tax-deductible donations; organizers may face personal liability exposure; harder to build institutional trust with larger partners.

**Responsibilities:** basic safety and honesty practices (venue agreements, privacy rules in this repo); clear communication that no nonprofit exists; careful handling of any money as personal funds with private records.

**Likely expenses:** none structural — only direct pilot costs (see [budget-template.md](../operations/budget-template.md)).

**Appropriate when:** validating demand with small pilots, costs are minimal and personally covered, and no grants or tax-deductible donations are sought. **This is the right model for the pilot phase.**

## Option 2 — Fiscal sponsorship through an established nonprofit

Operate under the legal and tax-exempt umbrella of an existing 501(c)(3) sponsor that receives funds on the project's behalf.

**Advantages:** tax-deductible donations and grant eligibility without forming a new entity; sponsor provides financial controls, compliance, and credibility; far faster and cheaper than independence; easier exit if the project pivots or winds down.

**Limitations:** sponsor charges administrative fees (percentage of revenue — verify with candidates); reduced autonomy (spending approvals, reporting, program alignment with sponsor's mission); the project does not own its exemption; finding a mission-aligned sponsor takes effort.

**Responsibilities:** written sponsorship agreement; regular financial reporting to the sponsor; compliance with sponsor policies (fundraising, lobbying limits, recordkeeping); maintaining the project's charitable purpose within the sponsor's mission.

**Likely expenses:** sponsor administrative fees; possible application fees; bookkeeping time; insurance as required by the sponsor. Amounts vary by sponsor — obtain written quotes, do not assume.

**Appropriate when:** pilots show demand, the project needs to receive donations or small grants, and building a new entity is not yet justified. **This is the likely next step after successful validation.**

## Option 3 — Independent Texas nonprofit corporation pursuing 501(c)(3) status

Form a Texas nonprofit corporation and apply to the IRS for federal tax-exempt recognition.

**Advantages:** full legal independence and ownership of mission, brand, and assets; direct grant and donation eligibility; strongest institutional credibility; governance tailored to the community served.

**Limitations:** highest cost, complexity, and timeline; ongoing governance burden (board, bylaws, filings, audits); state and federal compliance obligations every year; difficult to unwind; premature formalization can trap an unproven program in bureaucracy.

**Responsibilities:** Texas incorporation and registered agent; bylaws and board governance; conflict-of-interest policy; federal exemption application and annual filings; Texas Comptroller exemption filings; bookkeeping and financial controls; fundraising compliance; insurance; meeting minutes and recordkeeping. See [governance.md](governance.md) and [compliance-checklist.md](compliance-checklist.md).

**Likely expenses:** state filing fees, federal application fees, registered agent, insurance, bookkeeping/accounting support, and significant volunteer or staff time. **Do not rely on remembered fee amounts — verify current figures** at the Texas Secretary of State and IRS before budgeting.

**Appropriate when:** the program is validated, demand is sustained, funding requires an independent entity, and a committed board exists. **Not appropriate now — revisit only after pilots prove the model.**

## Comparison summary

| Factor | Informal | Fiscal sponsorship | Independent nonprofit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formation cost/complexity | None | Low–moderate | High |
| Tax-deductible donations | No | Yes (via sponsor) | Yes (once recognized) |
| Autonomy | Full (but no entity) | Shared with sponsor | Full |
| Compliance burden | Minimal | Moderate | High, permanent |
| Credibility with funders | Low | Good | Highest |
| Right time | Pilot/validation (now) | Post-validation growth | Sustained, funded scale |
