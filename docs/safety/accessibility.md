# Accessibility

> **DRAFT:** Proposed commitments. Unvalidated — review with participants and hosts after the pilot, including people with disabilities.

## Commitment

AI education that only reaches the already-able fails our mission. Accessibility here means: language, physical, sensory, cognitive, economic, and scheduling access — addressed in every workshop, not as an add-on.

## Language access

- Participant content is Spanish-first, in plain natural language; define every technical term.
- Host announcements go out in the community's language; provide bilingual support when a host community needs it.
- Avoid idioms and culture-specific references that don't travel; test materials with community readers.

## Physical and venue access

- Prefer step-free venues with accessible restrooms and seating; record gaps honestly in the [venue assessment](../../templates/venue-assessment.md) rather than hiding them.
- Arrange seating for mobility devices, late arrivals, and caregivers; keep aisles and exits clear.
- Confirm transport and parking realities with the host; schedule where the community already gathers.

## Sensory access

- Speak clearly with the room's acoustics in mind; use the speaker for any audio; face the group when explaining key steps.
- Minimum 14pt print on handouts; high-contrast slides; read aloud anything essential shown on screen.
- Offer large-print copies on request; budget for them (see [budget-template.md](../operations/budget-template.md)).

## Cognitive and learning access

- One step at a time; show-then-do; pairs and floating assistants; no timed pressure on beginners.
- Repeat core ideas across blocks (prompt → verify → privacy) rather than mentioning once.
- Literacy-aware design: read forms aloud, accept verbal feedback, use dictation-by-voice options.
- Rest breaks in every 90–120 minute session; plain-language take-home summaries.

## Economic access

- Free attendance; free tools only; no required purchases, accounts, or data-heavy apps.
- Paper alternatives for anyone without a device or connection; never single out who uses them.
- Schedule outside working hours; keep sessions single-visit (no multi-week prerequisite chains for core content).

## Scheduling and family access

- Evenings and weekends per host guidance; avoid major community and religious observances (ask the host).
- Welcome caregivers: note whether children may quietly accompany adults (venue-dependent; adults remain the participants).

## Feedback on accessibility

- Include access questions in debriefs: "¿Algo te dificultó participar hoy?" and review answers before the next session.
- Track accommodations requested vs. provided in the [pilot retrospective](../../templates/pilot-retrospective.md) (aggregate, anonymous).
