# Volunteer Onboarding

> **DRAFT:** Proposed roles and process. No volunteers recruited yet; revise after the first onboarding cycle.

## Proposed roles

| Role | Does | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead facilitator** | Delivers the workshop, owns timing, handles incidents | Full prep standard (see [facilitator-guide.md](facilitator-guide.md)); observe 1 + co-facilitate 1 before solo |
| **Assistant facilitator** | Floats to help stuck participants, tracks time, manages pairs | Read all workshop files; attend prep meeting |
| **Registration / welcome volunteer** | Greets, headcount sign-in (no names required), hands out materials, manages seating | Briefing + privacy rules; friendly Spanish |
| **Technical support volunteer** | Tests display/audio/internet, runs hotspot and demo phone, troubleshoots | Comfort with phones, projectors, hotspots |
| **Community outreach volunteer** | Helps identify hosts, translates/adapts announcements, follows up with partners | Community connections; bilingual helpful |

Small pilots may combine roles (e.g., assistant + tech, welcome + outreach), but **lead and assistant must always be two different people** so help never stops the session.

## Recruitment (proposed)

- Ask host organizations for trusted bilingual helpers; invite strong participants from earlier workshops to assist at later ones.
- Use [volunteer-interest.md](../../templates/volunteer-interest.md) to collect expressions of interest — store responses outside this public repository.
- Look for patience and reliability over technical depth; teaching beginners is the skill that matters most.
- No one facilitates solo without completing orientation below.

## Orientation (required for all roles)

1. Read: project README, [facilitator-guide.md](facilitator-guide.md), and the workshop files for the assigned session.
2. Attend the 30-minute prep meeting (roles, agenda walk, demo test, emergency basics).
3. Confirm understanding of the privacy rules and conduct expectations (below).
4. New leads: observe one workshop, co-facilitate one, then lead with a mentor present.

## Instructional consistency

- Volunteers deliver the written curriculum as-is; adaptations limited to the approved list in the facilitator guide.
- Debrief after every workshop: what worked, what confused participants, timing notes. Feed improvements back as issues or doc updates — never as silent personal variations.

## Privacy expectations

- Volunteers never collect, photograph, or share participant names, contacts, screens, or responses.
- Volunteer contact details are operational data: kept private, never committed to this repo.
- Breach procedure: stop the exposure, tell the lead immediately, document privately, fix the process.

## Conduct considerations

- Volunteers represent a nonsectarian, non-commercial educational initiative: no selling, recruiting, preaching, or campaigning at events.
- Report concerns about participant safety or volunteer behavior to the lead facilitator promptly and confidentially.
- A formal code of conduct and reporting channel will be added in a later setup phase; until then the lead facilitator is the point of contact, and serious concerns pause the volunteer's participation pending review.

## Recognition and retention (proposed)

- Thank volunteers publicly (with consent) and privately after every session.
- Share aggregate impact with the team so helpers see the difference they make.
- Keep asks small and scheduled: no surprise commitments, no guilt-driven coverage.
