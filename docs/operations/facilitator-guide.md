# Facilitator Guide (General Standards)

> **DRAFT:** Proposed cross-workshop standards. Workshop-specific notes live in each `workshops/0X-*/facilitator-notes.md`.

## Who this is for

Any volunteer who leads or assists a workshop. Read this plus the specific workshop's facilitator notes before facilitating.

## Teaching principles for beginners

1. **Spanish-first, plain language.** No unexplained jargon; define every technical term (prompt, alucinación, navegador) and repeat it.
2. **Show, then do.** Demonstrate each step on the big screen before asking participants to try it.
3. **Pairs from minute one.** Seat people in twos; neighbors help neighbors; assistants float.
4. **Practice over lecture.** If time runs short, cut explanation — never hands-on practice.
5. **Celebrate small wins.** Name progress out loud ("¡ya hicieron su primer prompt!"). Many participants arrive convinced technology "isn't for them."
6. **No one left behind visibly.** The 2-minute rule: if someone is stuck, an assistant helps at their seat while the group continues.

## Instructional consistency

- Follow the written `curriculum.md` for timing and core content. Do not freelance the lesson plan.
- Approved adaptations (no permission needed): extra examples, slower pacing, reordered practice pairs, additional time on the topic the group finds hardest.
- Changes to learning objectives, privacy rules, or feedback questions require team discussion first.
- After each workshop, file observations (what dragged, what confused, timing drift) so the curriculum improves from evidence.

## Privacy expectations (non-negotiable)

- Fictional examples only in all demos and exercises. Intervene kindly and immediately if real personal data appears.
- Never photograph participant screens, ask to see accounts, or collect names, addresses, or contact details.
- Feedback is anonymous. Paper forms are stored outside this public repository and summarized only in aggregate.
- Report any privacy incident (real data exposed, photos taken without consent) to the lead immediately.

## Conduct expectations

- This is education, not sales, recruiting, or ministry: no product pitches, no job promises, no religious content, no political messaging — regardless of venue.
- Treat every participant with patience and respect; never mock a question or rush a struggling learner.
- Do not ask about immigration status, income, health, or other sensitive matters.
- Physical safety first: know the exits, the safety contact, and the venue's emergency basics.
- A formal code of conduct will be added in a later setup phase; until then these standards plus common decency govern, and the lead facilitator may remove anyone disrupting participant safety or learning.

## Preparation standard

- Read the workshop's `README.md`, `curriculum.md`, `facilitator-notes.md`, and `exercises.md` end to end.
- Run every prompt on a phone yourself; note where AI responses differ from the plan.
- New facilitators: observe one full workshop and co-facilitate one before leading solo.
- Arrive 45 minutes early; test display, audio, internet, and backup hotspot.
