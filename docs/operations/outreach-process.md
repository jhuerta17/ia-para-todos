# Outreach Process

> **DRAFT:** Proposed repeatable process. No hosts contacted yet; refine after the first conversations.

## Goal

Find trusted host organizations, confirm real audience interest, and agree on clear responsibilities — without overpromising. Follows the lifecycle in [workshop-checklist.md](workshop-checklist.md) (steps 1–6, 12).

## Step 1 — Identify candidates

- Start where trust already exists: Hispanic churches known to the team, then libraries, community centers, schools, nonprofits.
- Good-fit signals: serves Spanish-speaking adults, has a usable room, communicates regularly with its community, leadership is curious (not necessarily technical).
- Keep a private tracker (outside this repo): organization, contact person, status, next action. Never commit it.

## Step 2 — Make first contact

- Prefer a warm introduction (a member or volunteer who attends there) over a cold message.
- Use [partnership-outreach.md](../../templates/partnership-outreach.md); adapt to the channel (email, WhatsApp, in-person visit).
- Ask for a 20-minute conversation with a decision-maker, not a commitment.

## Step 3 — Explain the mission (the conversation)

Cover, in this order:

1. **What:** free 90–120 minute beginner AI workshops in Spanish, smartphone-only, no paid tools.
2. **Who:** their community members — parents, workers, older adults, job seekers.
3. **Character:** inclusive, nonsectarian, non-commercial; no sales, no religious content, no data collection.
4. **Ask:** a room, help announcing, and a present contact person. We bring facilitators and materials.
5. **Honesty:** this is a pilot — we're validating, not launching; small groups (10–20); we'll share aggregate results.

Leave a one-page summary if helpful (adapt the outreach template). Answer "what's the catch?" directly: there is none; costs are covered by the organizing team during the pilot.

## Step 4 — Confirm audience and attendance

- Ask: who would come, how many realistically, best days/times, childcare or transport constraints.
- Discount enthusiasm honestly: a leader's "everyone will come!" is not a headcount. Ask how they announce events and what turnout similar events get.
- Agree on a realistic target and a promotion plan with dates.

## Step 5 — Assess the venue

- Walk the space with [venue-assessment.md](../../templates/venue-assessment.md): internet, display, outlets, audio, seating, accessibility, exits.
- Decide on offline backups if internet is weak (hotspot + paper activities).
- Confirm capacity and room setup (pairs seating preferred).

## Step 6 — Establish responsibilities

- Confirm the split in [venue-requirements.md](venue-requirements.md): space, outreach, safety contact, insurance clarity.
- Record the agreement (email confirmation is fine) and store privately.
- Exchange day-of contact numbers (private; never in the repo).

## Step 7 — Follow up with the partner (after the workshop)

- Thank them within 48 hours; share aggregate outcomes (attendance count, completion signals, anonymous satisfaction).
- Ask for their honest feedback: what did they hear from attendees? Would they host again? What would they change?
- Record lessons in [pilot-retrospective.md](../../templates/pilot-retrospective.md) (anonymized before anything touches the repo).
- Only discuss a next session after the debrief — no standing commitments from enthusiasm alone.

## Principles

- Never imply nonprofit status, funding, certification, or scale we don't have.
- Never use partner access for commercial purposes or share attendee access with third parties.
- Small and honest beats big and vague: one validated host is worth ten "maybes."
